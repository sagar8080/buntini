# Buntini privacy policy

*Last updated: November 16, 2026. This describes what the app does; it is not legal advice. Have it reviewed before a commercial launch.*

Buntini is a desktop companion that runs on your Mac. There are no Buntini servers and no Buntini account. We (the people who make Buntini) never receive your messages, files, settings or usage.

## What stays on your Mac

Buntini keeps its records in its folder under `~/Library/Application Support/app.buntini.companion` and in your macOS Keychain. Its separate browser profiles also use macOS-managed WebKit storage:

- **Your conversation threads and task journals**, including full saved model messages, step inputs, approvals, tool evidence, destination IDs, receipts and which local-data categories were used. These let Buntini resume a task without repeating its completed actions. Also your settings, saved timers, searches, price watches, and the things you ask it to remember. Timers and monitors keep due times, execution/delivery status and errors. Approved intent records retain monitor queries/pages/settings and approved memory facts/timer durations to prevent a repeated approval from duplicating or recreating something you deleted, forgot or cancelled; these remain until Delete everything.
- **The file index** for folders you choose to allow. Buntini reads only those folders, and you can remove access at any time. It brings the index up to date on its own, shortly after launch and every six hours, pausing while you're chatting or the Mac is on battery. For PDF, Word, RTF and Pages files it keeps the text Buntini's Apple helper read from them on your Mac (up to 64 KB each); while it reads, the helper is cut off from the network, from other apps (no Apple events, no opening links or apps) and from your folders and the Mac's temporary and cache folders, so a document can't reach out through it. It can still read the system's own files, which the document readers need. Text read during a search is also kept in memory until Buntini quits, so an unchanged document isn't read again.
- **What you're doing:** if "Notice what I'm doing" is on, the name of the app in front, how long you've been active, window positions (never titles or contents) and battery, CPU and disk levels. This is kept in memory for at most three hours and never saved. What a model was given for a task is also saved in that task's record, until you clear history.
- **The memory index:** a searchable copy, on your Mac, of what Buntini may already read: things you asked it to remember, earlier conversations, and, for each app you allowed, Notes changed in the last year, inbox email from the last 90 days (subject, sender and the first 500 characters), calendar events from 30 days back to 60 days ahead, open reminders, and text from shared folders already in the file index. Each entry keeps its data categories. Meaning-based search uses a small search model that ships inside Buntini (bge-small-en-v1.5) and runs on your Mac; nothing is downloaded or sent anywhere to build or search it. Anything that looks like a password, credential, API key or private key is left out of the index. A cloud brain only gets a result when every category in it is allowed in Privacy. Switching an app off, removing a folder, forgetting a fact or clearing history removes the matching entries; Delete everything removes the whole index. An index update already under way when you do this can't put the entries back: it checks for deletions before it writes and drops what it read. Building the index never opens Mail or Notes: if one is closed, Buntini leaves it closed and keeps what it indexed from it before.
- **People from Contacts:** if you allow Contacts, Buntini looks up only the people you name (for example who to address a draft to), at most five matches at a time. The names, email addresses and phone numbers it found are saved with that task like any other read. Contacts itself is not read into the memory index, but a finished conversation that showed contact details is remembered like any other conversation, tagged as Contacts data, so only brains allowed to see Contacts can search it.
- **What “this” means:** when you open Buntini (⌘⇧Space, the menu bar or the pet), it notes the app you were in and, if Accessibility is allowed, that app's front window title and any text you had selected. Password fields are never read. This is kept in memory for at most ten minutes, and only until you switch to another app, and never saved unless a request about “this” uses it; that task then records it like any other read. Only a request that points at something on screen uses it (“this email”, “reply to her”), not “this weekend” or “her birthday”. The email open in Mail, the page in your browser or the files selected in Finder are read only when a request refers to them, and Mail and browsers must be allowed in Settings → Apps. Nothing, not even a window title or selected text, is read from a browser you haven't allowed or from a private window where the browser says which windows are private (not Safari). A cloud brain sees selected text only with **What you select** enabled in Privacy, and an email, page or file names only with the matching category enabled. Every answer records each kind of data it shows (“My day” can be Calendar, Mail and System at once), and a cloud brain is only shown an earlier answer when it is allowed all of them.
- **Emails “What needs a reply?” listed:** Mail’s ids, Message-IDs, senders, subjects and dates of only the emails that answer named, so “reply to the second one” can answer exactly that email. One list is kept at a time, used for 12 hours in the same conversation, and removed when you delete that turn or clear history.
- **Heads-ups**, only if you turn them on: each heads-up (the event's title, time, place and invited people's names; the reminders' titles and due times; or the emails' senders, subjects, dates and Mail ids), kept until two days after it stops being relevant (once you open one, its conversation turn keeps what it said, and deleting that turn removes the heads-up too), plus a list of what was already mentioned, kept as one-way hashes without any text for 30 days, and when each kind was last given, so nothing repeats. The most recent inbox sort is also kept in memory for up to 30 minutes, never on disk. Clearing history or switching heads-ups off removes their alerts, and clearing history removes the heads-ups; Delete everything removes the hashes too. A heads-up's alert names only its kind, never a title, name or place.
- **API keys** you add, and your **ChatGPT sign-in** if you use one, in the macOS Keychain.
- **What you attach to a message:** files you add with the paperclip, drop on the chat or the cat, or send from the island's Chat button are read when you send the message, in the same sandboxed helper and with the same limits as shared folders (up to 64 KB of text each), wherever the file is. The text is saved with that task like any other read. A window you let the cat go over is read once, then: its title, its address if it shows a web page, and up to 24,000 characters of its text, through Accessibility. It is kept in memory only while its chip shows in the chat (an hour at most) and is never saved unless you send it. Nothing is read from a browser you haven't allowed, from a browser window that isn't the front one (Buntini can only check the front one is not private), from a private window, from Firefox or other browsers whose private windows it can't tell apart, from password fields or from password managers, and Mail only once it's allowed. Pictures are kept in memory for that task's steps (an hour at most) and never saved; an iPhone picture (HEIC) or a very large one is first made into a smaller JPEG on this Mac with macOS's `sips`, in Buntini's cache, and deleted straight away.
- **Command-line tools used as the brain** keep their own records as they do in a terminal: Buntini asks Claude Code and Codex not to save the session, and Copilot CLI keeps its session history in `~/.copilot` as it always does. A picture for one of them is written to a private folder in Buntini's cache for that run and removed when it ends.
- **Tools you added (MCP servers):** the name you use for each, the command that starts it, and the tools it listed, in Buntini's database on this Mac; the values you put before that command (such as a token) are kept in the macOS Keychain, with only their names in the database. Those values go only to that server's process; they are never written to the activity log, shown again in Settings or included in an export (only their names are). Secrets passed in the command itself (such as `--token …` or a password in a connection URL) are hidden in Settings and in the export. The server's own messages to its error output are discarded, not kept.
- **The cat's mood:** how lively, affectionate, bored or cross it feels and how close it has grown to you, worked out from how you pet, click and talk to it, so it carries over between launches. It is six numbers and the time they were last saved, in Buntini's own window storage, with no words, pointer positions or record of what you did. To react to a hello or a thank-you, Buntini checks the words of each message you send against a short list on your Mac; that check keeps nothing. Delete everything resets the mood.
- **Mac history:** one point a minute with CPU use, memory pressure and battery level, kept for 24 hours, plus how much space your apps, caches and shared folders take, measured once a day. No app names are in it. A Mac at a glance card you ask for is kept with that message, including the names of the apps it shows. All of it is in Export my data and goes with Delete everything.
- **Coding agents**, only if you switch Claude Code or Codex on in Settings → Apps: Buntini adds its hook entries to that agent’s settings file, after saving a copy of the file as it was under `agent-backups` in its folder (the last ten per agent). The agent then tells Buntini, through a socket on this Mac that only your account can open, when a turn starts and ends, the project folder, and what it asks permission for (the command, file, address or tool details, or its questions and choices). Buntini reads the first line of the agent’s final reply to say it’s done and ignores the rest, including your prompt’s text. Requests are held in memory only while they’re showing and are never saved or given to a model, on-device or cloud. A small marker file notes that an agent has reached Buntini once.
  - The same goes for Copilot CLI (a hook file of Buntini’s own, `~/.copilot/hooks/buntini.json`), Gemini CLI (`~/.gemini/settings.json`), Antigravity (a `buntini` entry in `~/.gemini/config/hooks.json`), and plugin files of Buntini’s own for OpenCode, Amp and Hermes. Cursor runs Claude Code’s hooks itself. While a session works, its hooks also tell Buntini each tool it uses with that tool’s details (a command, a file path, an edit’s old and new text) and which terminal app it runs in. The step list and line counts are kept in memory for the island and gone after fifteen quiet minutes.
  - **Your week with the agents** is counted on this Mac in `agents-recap.json` in Buntini’s folder: time, turns, sessions, files and lines changed, commands, what you allowed or denied, and project folder names. Twelve weeks are kept. **Share** saves a picture to Downloads without project names; nothing is sent.
  - **Plan usage:** switching on Plan usage for Claude Code wraps its status line, keeping yours, so Buntini gets the plan windows Claude Code already shows it; your own status line is put back when you switch it off. For Codex, Buntini asks the Codex app on this Mac (`codex app-server`), at most once a minute after a turn, which asks OpenAI with your Codex sign-in; only the windows come back to Buntini.
- **Mail changes**, if you allow Mail in Settings → Apps and use the mail card or Settings → Apps → Mail: each change Buntini made in Mail (marked read or unread, flagged, junk, moved, a draft or reply saved), with the email's Message-ID, sender, subject and the mailboxes involved, and a draft's recipients and attachment names, so Undo can put it back. The last 500 are kept in `mail/log.json`; Delete everything removes it. Your VIPs, banned words and sorting rules are kept with your settings. Files you drop to attach, and the pages of a PDF split for a mail merge, are copied into Buntini's folder for Mail to attach, and removed the first time Buntini starts after a day; Mail keeps its own copy in the draft. Buntini never opens Mail to read it, and reads it in the background only while Mail is open and already allowed, so it never asks for permission on its own. Nothing is ever deleted from Mail and nothing is sent: replies and merge mail stay drafts until you send them.
- **What's playing**, if you allow Music, Spotify or TV in Settings → Apps: the track, artist, album, position, shuffle, repeat and volume of a player that's already open, read about once a second while the island shows it and less often otherwise, never while the screen sleeps or is locked, and kept in memory only. A player that isn't open is left closed, and one macOS hasn't let Buntini ask yet is left alone. The covers of the last few tracks are kept in `covers` in Buntini's cache folder so the island can draw them; the folder is emptied each time Buntini starts.
- **An activity log** of tools used and permissions given, and a **crash log** (time, error and code location only).
- **Notifications held by macOS**, only if desktop notifications are on: each pending timer’s alert (“Your 10 minute timer is done.”), so it can appear after you quit, and an alert when a task waits for your OK. An approval alert names only the kind of work, such as “Buntini drafted an email and needs your OK.”, and never a recipient, title, time or text, because it can show on the lock screen. macOS keeps these in Notification Center until they are shown, cleared or withdrawn; cancelling a timer, or choosing Allow or Cancel on the proposal’s sheet, stopping its task or clearing history, withdraws its alert.
- **Open at login**, if you turn it on, is a login item registered with macOS for Buntini. You can also remove it in System Settings → General → Login Items.
- **Your voice**, only after you tap or hold the mic, and while a voice conversation you started keeps listening. On macOS 26, Apple’s on-device speech recognition turns it into words as you talk and no audio is written to disk. Where that isn’t available (an older macOS or a language Apple’s recognizer doesn’t support), a private recording of up to 30 seconds per turn is kept until it is transcribed on this Mac, then deleted after transcription, failure or cancellation. In a conversation the microphone also stays on while Buntini reads its answer, so you can talk over it; Buntini keeps the text of what it just said in memory for at most a minute to tell its own voice from yours. Audio never leaves your Mac and is never sent to a cloud brain; only the words you said are sent, like a typed message.
- **Dictation anywhere**, only if you turn it on: while you hold the dictation key (or after a double tap, until you press it again), the microphone's sound is kept in memory only, never on disk, until the words are ready, then dropped. The words are typed where your cursor is through the clipboard; whatever the clipboard held is put back 0.7 seconds later unless something else changed it meanwhile, and the dictated words are marked so clipboard managers skip them. Something a password manager put on the clipboard (marked concealed) is never put back: the clipboard is cleared instead. The words are typed only into the same field of the app the dictation started in, checked again right before pasting; where the cursor isn't in a text field, has moved to another field or app, or the field doesn't show the words arriving, they stay on the clipboard instead. A hands-free dictation still open after 10 minutes ends by itself, and its words only go to the clipboard. To pick the style and where the words go, Buntini reads the name of the app in front and the kind of field the cursor is in (text area, password field and so on). To see whether the words arrived, it compares the field's length, the caret's position and a hash of its text from just before and after pasting; the text is read only to make that hash, in memory, and never kept. Words said at a password field, or while macOS's secure input is on (a password being typed anywhere, or a terminal's Secure Keyboard Entry), are dropped: not typed, not put on the clipboard, not kept, and not given to a brain. The key watcher learns only that the dictation key, Esc or some other key went down, never which key, and only in the minutes after you used the dictation key. Buntini keeps each dictation's words, the app's name, the word count and how long you spoke for 30 days, for the Library's history and ⌃⌘V; after that only the counts stay, for the statistics (older words are cleared at launch and every hour). Clear history and forgetting a dictation remove its words, from ⌃⌘V too; Delete everything removes all of it. While you dictate, outputs that weren't already muted are muted and then unmuted; their device ids are kept until then, so if Buntini or its audio helper stops unexpectedly, a new helper unmutes them straight away, or Buntini does at the next launch.
- **Your words** (dictionary, replacements and snippets) are part of your settings.
- **Screenshots and captures**, only when you press a capture shortcut: macOS asks once for Screen Recording at the first one. Each picture is saved in `captures` in Buntini's cache folder (`~/Library/Caches/app.buntini.companion`) so it can be dragged out, pasted or marked up, and is deleted after a day (at most the newest 40 are kept). A screenshot is also put on your clipboard; pasting from the island puts your clipboard back as it was (except something a password manager put there, which is cleared instead). Dictating pastes a capture with your words only while its card is showing on the island and the dictation started over it; never a whole-screen screenshot, and never a capture you pasted, copied, closed or that has left the island. Nothing is read from a picture and none is sent anywhere unless you choose **Ask**, which attaches it in the chat like a file you attach. Your shortcuts, a running focus timer (its phase and deadline) and whether the island is muted are kept with your settings.
- **Call notes**, only if you turn on Record calls and start a recording: the microphone and everything the Mac plays are recorded to two private files in Buntini's cache folder. A red mark and a timer show on the dictation pill and the island the whole time. Every 20 seconds the latest stretch is turned into words for the Library. When you stop, the speakers' sound heard by the microphone is removed, the other side is told apart by voice, and the lines are saved with the note; the audio files are deleted as soon as those lines are saved, and any left behind by a crash are deleted at the next launch. Notes stay until you delete them, clear history or delete everything. Recording a call can be subject to consent laws where you or the other people are; it's your choice to make.
- **“Hey Buntini”**, only if you turn it on: the microphone stays on (macOS shows its orange dot), but nothing is recognized until someone speaks; then that stretch, with the half second before it, is turned into words on this Mac and checked for the phrase. Nothing is kept: no audio, no words. Logs carry only counts. It stops while the Mac sleeps or is locked, in Low Power Mode, while you dictate and while a voice turn (by the wake word or the mic button) or call recording has the microphone.
- **Speech models**: Parakeet, the speaker diarizer and the voice detector are kept in `speech-models` in Buntini's folder once you download them, and removed when you remove them. Recognition, diarization and voice detection run on this Mac.
- **Separate browser profiles**, one per approved HTTPS origin, retain that site's cookies, sign-ins and website storage across restarts. They do not use Safari, Chrome or Buntini's main interface profile. Approved reads retain the page address, title and up to 6,000 characters of visible page text in task history. Form fields, passwords and editable text are excluded from that extraction. Website text remains untrusted content.
- **Web research records**, including the original request, approved query, selected source addresses, bounded page text, route attempts and missing-evidence status, are saved with the task. Research creates and reads a dedicated tab in an allowed existing/default browser. It does not read other tabs' titles, addresses or page contents, or read cookie files, but the browser may use its normal cookies and sign-ins and record those visits in its history. Browser-derived evidence retains its Browser data category in saved answers and task journals.

On-device brains (Apple Intelligence and local MLX models) can use all of this, because none of it leaves your Mac.

For each call: the tool's name and exactly the details shown on the approval sheet (a tool you switched on to run without asking skips the sheet only when every detail it gets is in your own request; details taken from your screen, an email or an earlier reply are always shown first). The server is software you chose and may send what it receives elsewhere, as its own documentation describes. ## Your controls

- **Settings → Privacy** lists what Buntini notices, which folders it can read, what cloud models may see, and what it remembers. You can switch each of these off or remove items.
- **Clear conversation history** (in History) asks you to confirm, then erases every conversation and task journal, the file index, and their entries in the memory index. The file index is rebuilt from the folders you still allow at its next update (shortly after launch and every six hours); remove a folder in Privacy → Folders to keep its text out. Settings, folders, lists, tools you added and the things you asked Buntini to remember stay.
- **Export** saves all conversation turns, thread metadata/membership, task journals, settings, lists, memories, timers, monitor run/delivery history and approved monitor and local-control intent records as a JSON file. Saved browser readbacks in task history are included. Tools you added are exported as their names, commands and the names of their values. Keys, the values you gave tools you added, browser cookies, sign-in sessions and website cache/storage are not exported.
- **Delete everything** closes the separate browser and asks WebKit to delete each of its profiles, stops any tools you added that are running, stops dictation, the wake word and any call recording (deleting its audio) and unmutes what dictation muted, deletes captures, then erases the conversation, settings, folders, lists, memories, timers, monitor history and intent records, tools you added and their values, file index, saved keys and recordings, removes every Buntini alert macOS still holds, scheduled or shown, removes Buntini from your login items, resets the cat's mood, and starts setup again. A settings change that was still waiting to save is dropped rather than written back afterwards. If WebKit cannot confirm browser profile removal, deletion reports an error and retains the profile identifiers so it can be retried. It does not erase history, cookies or sign-ins in your existing browser.
- Deletion applies to Buntini's current app data and cache, including the current database and its journal files. If you upgraded from the former Nexon app, migration preserves the original `~/Library/Application Support/app.nexon.companion` folder as a separate backup; Delete everything does not remove that legacy folder. Copies you exported, destination records created in other apps, Time Machine backups and APFS snapshots also remain. Deletion is logical removal, not a guarantee of forensic erasure from your disk or backups.
- **Copy support info** copies a short summary you can choose to share with us when reporting a bug: app and macOS versions, which brain is set up, recent activity entries and crashes, with your home folder shortened to `~`. It contains no messages, file contents or keys.

Buntini has no analytics and no telemetry, and it doesn't send crash reports automatically.
