# Buntini user guide

Everything Buntini does, in detail. For a quick overview, see the [README](../README.md).

## First launch

Buntini opens its bubble and walks you through setup as a short conversation: what to call you, its name and coat, which brain to use, an optional Brave or Tavily key with free-tier mode and a daily cap, and whether it may roam and notice what you're doing. Keys go straight to the macOS Keychain. You can skip setup, and **Settings → Run setup again** starts it over. Anyone who already chatted with an earlier version skips it automatically.

## Choose a brain

Settings → Brain picks the model that answers. Every backend gets the same validated tools and the same conversation memory.

- **Apple Intelligence** (default): Apple's on-device Foundation Models through the `native/buntini-fm` helper. On-device, with nothing to download. It needs macOS 26 on Apple silicon with Apple Intelligence turned on; Settings explains what to do when it isn't available. Its context is small (about 4K tokens), so Buntini gives it compact tool results and a shorter memory.
- **Local MLX model:** a Qwen3.5 or Gemma 4 model run by MLX on your Mac, fully offline. Brain → Local model shows **Recommended for this Mac**: it reads the chip and memory and suggests a model that fits, keeping it under about half of memory so your apps stay comfortable.

  | Mac | Best fit | Also fits |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 8–15 GB | Qwen3.5 4B (3.1 GB) | Gemma 4 E2B (3.6 GB) |
  | 16–35 GB, base M-series chip | Gemma 4 E4B (5.2 GB), quicker on lower memory bandwidth | Qwen3.5 9B (6.0 GB) |
  | 16–35 GB, Pro, Max or Ultra | Qwen3.5 9B (6.0 GB) | Gemma 4 E4B (5.2 GB) |
  | 36 GB or more | Qwen3.5 35B-A3B (20.4 GB), fast for its size | Gemma 4 31B (18.4 GB) on Max and Ultra chips, otherwise Qwen3.5 9B |

  It gives the three Terminal commands to copy (create a Python environment, install MLX LM, download the model with `hf`), ticks each step off as it notices it, and fills in the Python and model folder fields once the download is complete. Buntini never installs packages or downloads models itself. Developers can also preset `BUNTINI_PYTHON` and `BUNTINI_MODEL_PATH`. Existing installs that already used MLX stay on it.
- **Cloud model:** Anthropic (suggested `claude-opus-5-5`), OpenAI (`gpt-5-mini`) or Google Gemini (`gemini-2.5-flash`) with your own API key; any model name your key can use works. Task answers are checked before they appear, so unverified action narration is withheld. Each message reserves a cautious cost estimate against your **monthly spending cap** before it is sent, then settles to the token usage the provider reports, at the per-million-token prices shown in Settings (suggested from published October 2026 prices; check your provider's page, their invoice is authoritative). Rejected requests are refunded, Buntini warns once at 80% and stops at the cap. A cloud model sees only what you allow under Settings → Privacy (see Privacy below).
- **ChatGPT:** choose ChatGPT under Cloud model and press **Sign in with ChatGPT**. Your browser opens to OpenAI's sign-in, you allow Buntini to use your plan, and the browser hands the result back to Buntini on `127.0.0.1`. Each person uses their own ChatGPT account and plan; there's no API key and no per-message cost, and OpenAI sets the limits (Buntini explains when one is reached). The sign-in is kept in the Keychain and renewed automatically; Sign out revokes it with OpenAI. This uses OpenAI's [Sign in with ChatGPT flow for open-source, locally hosted apps](https://developers.openai.com/siwc/token-sharing-open-source), which registers Buntini on first sign-in; it's a preview, so details may change.

Earlier MLX and Apple probes are recorded in [acceptance evidence](acceptance.md). The current Apple multi-step routing gate failed 4 of 9 phases, including an incorrect derived reminder time. Treat complex tasks as requiring careful field review; see [scenario evaluations](evaluations.md).

## Everyday use

- **The companion:** drag the character to move it; click to open or tuck away the bubble. To quit, choose Quit Buntini in the companion’s ⋯ menu, press ⌘Q while the bubble is open, or right-click the menu bar icon (which can also show or hide Buntini). It floats above other windows and follows desktop Spaces.
- **Ask anything:** messages are saved inside conversation threads. History shows one entry per conversation, with its title and turn count; reopening it restores that thread and subsequent messages continue it. **New topic** starts a separate empty conversation. **Load earlier** retrieves older turns in the selected thread. Models receive the recent context that fits their budget and privacy permissions. [Legacy history migration](conversations.md) preserves existing turns without inventing missing boundaries.
- **Saved task progress:** a multi-step request keeps its requested outcomes, steps, approved inputs and results in SQLite. Approving a step resumes the same task. After a restart, review pending approvals or continue a paused task; fill in missing fields such as a deadline, or use Additional task details. **Waiting for you**, at the top of the chat, lists every proposal still waiting for your OK, from any conversation; Review opens its sheet in that conversation. If Buntini is hidden or tucked away when a task stops at a proposal, and desktop notifications are on, you get a notification such as “Buntini drafted an email and needs your OK.” It never shows the recipient, title or text. Review opens the same sheet; Later leaves it waiting. Nothing can be approved from the notification. Task progress shows unfinished results separately from completed steps. Inspect inputs and result shows where proposed values came from and any approval edits. Completed record writes retain their destination IDs and verified fields. An interrupted or uncertain write is marked unknown and cannot replay automatically. Stop cancels future steps, without undoing completed actions. Tasks currently have a 12-step limit.
- **My files:** add a folder in Settings first. Search matches names and small text documents within that scope. Revoke access at any time. Hidden files and symlinks are skipped; scans are bounded to 10,000 entries, 40 hits, and 64 KB per text file.
- **My Mac:** read CPU, memory, storage, process usage, and supported FileVault/Gatekeeper/SIP status. These tools work without a model or network. Buntini does not claim the OS is up to date or perform security changes.
- **Online lookups:** a direct web-research request follows one route: browser search, selected source reads, and a cited answer, with the configured search API as fallback. Other questions may use a specific service: weather (Open-Meteo), exchange rates (ECB via Frankfurter), Wikipedia, nearby places (OpenStreetMap/Nominatim), eBay listings or Duffel flight offers. Before anything is sent, an approval sheet shows the service, host and exact outgoing values; you can allow it once or cancel. Weather, rates, Wikipedia and places need no key. eBay and Duffel credentials are optional, kept in macOS Keychain, and their searches count separately toward request caps. Nothing is bought or booked. See [online tools](online-tools.md).
- **Buntini browser workspace:** approve an HTTPS page to open and read it in a separate persistent profile on macOS 14+. Each origin has its own sign-ins and storage. Cross-origin navigation, popups and downloads are blocked; manual sign-in is possible where those limits allow it. Buntini reads bounded visible text and cannot fill forms, buy, book or send. This path needs Browser sharing for cloud brains; Delete everything removes its WebKit profiles.
- **The web:** ask “search the web for…” or “google…” to start research. Buntini searches Google in a dedicated tab in an allowed existing/default browser, reads relevant public result pages in that tab, and checks that its answer cites pages it actually read. Enable the browser in Settings → Apps and allow browser automation when prompted. Native research-tab reading supports Chrome, Brave and Edge, with existing Accessibility access as an alternative when page scripting is disabled. Other browsers can open Google and use API/public-HTML fallback. Your browser may use its existing cookies and sign-ins; a cloud brain receives browser-derived results only with Browser sharing enabled. A Brave or Tavily key is optional fallback when the browser cannot gather enough evidence; recurring saved searches still need that provider key, and History asks you to add it before you can save one. The one-use approval shows the query, browser search and API fallback before dispatch. Up to six selected source pages may be read, with a public HTML read if browser rendering fails. The original request, sources, retrieval times, attempts and missing evidence are saved. Opening results alone never completes the research. If source coverage or the summary is incomplete, Buntini says so. Search matching and citation checks do not guarantee factual correctness.
- **History → Scheduled work:** see saved timers and recurring searches/price watches, including the last successful check, next due time, late runs and alert failures. Ask “keep checking this query every hour” or “track this product hourly below $50” to create a monitor through an editable approval in chat. The sheet authorizes the exact recurring query/page and interval; search checks use your configured allowance or budget. Creation verifies the stored configuration and gives it a stable ID, without fetching a price or search result. You can pause, check now, or delete monitors; intervals range from 15 minutes to one year. Timers survive restart and can be cancelled individually here; say “cancel all timers” to cancel every pending timer. Closing the window keeps Buntini running. With desktop notifications on, macOS also holds each timer’s alert, so it can still appear after you quit Buntini; cancelling the timer removes it. Checks wait until Buntini returns, and sleep delays both; when your Mac wakes, Buntini catches up at once. Overdue work runs once, and the status records the delay. A timer alert macOS already showed while Buntini was quit is not repeated, unless you cleared it from Notification Center. To have Buntini running after a restart, turn on **Open at login** in Settings → Buntini; it works in the installed app, and macOS may ask you to allow it in System Settings → General → Login Items. Desktop notifications are optional and require a user-initiated macOS permission request. Failed alerts remain visible. If the app closes during an alert, its delivery is marked unknown and the alert is not repeated automatically.
- **Voice:** tap the microphone button, speak, and tap it again; or hold it while you speak and let go. Buntini transcribes on your Mac, sends what it heard and answers out loud (turn off “Answer out loud when I talk” in Settings → Buntini to keep it quiet). Recordings stop after 30 seconds. If the microphone only delivers silence, Buntini says so instead of guessing. `Esc` cancels audio; Read aloud uses macOS speech and can be interrupted.

## Personalize your assistant

Settings include a local profile: assistant name, personality instructions and presets, two characters (a customizable cat by default, or orbit), cat coat (seven, including tabby, tuxedo, calico, and siamese), eye color, accessory (bell collar, bow, bandana, top hat, or flower crown, tinted with the accent color), motion personality (calm, playful, or sleepy; reduced motion still wins; the cat plays with a ball of wool tinted with the accent color, at random: batting it, pouncing on it or nudging it, each time with a different strength; playful cats play most and often come back for more, sleepy ones only give it a lazy nudge), accent color, companion size, installed macOS speaking voice and pace, theme, and reduced motion. The saved profile changes the desktop character and the trusted model context. Custom personality text never grants tools or permissions.

Settings save on their own a moment after you stop editing, and straight away when you close Settings (Esc included). While a task is running, changes to the brain, apps or online limits wait for it to finish, then save; Settings shows “Saves after this task” meanwhile, and a change that can’t be saved says why instead of looking saved. Any accent colour you pick is darkened or lightened just enough to stay readable (4.5:1) on the panel, and text on it turns white or dark to match. Text size also scales Settings, approval sheets and the small labels around the composer. Messages appear above the composer, so they never cover Send or the mic, and an error already shown in the conversation isn’t repeated.

**Out and about.** Buntini walks along the bottom of the screen, hops up to sit on top of the window you are using and rides along when you move it, and falls back down (or onto the window below) when you drop it or close its perch. It naps when you have been away for three minutes, greets you when you come back, and turns invisible and click-through during meetings (Zoom, Teams, FaceTime, Webex) and full-screen apps. Helpful nudges appear as a small speech bubble with one-click actions: a stretch break after about 50 active minutes (snooze 15 min), a 25-minute focus timer offered in code, writing and design apps, “Look it up?” in a browser, “Find a file?” in Finder, a late-night wind-down reminder, and battery, CPU and disk warnings with a “Check my Mac” shortcut. Nudges are spaced at least ten minutes apart and each kind has its own cooldown. The signals are seconds since your last input, the frontmost app's name, window frames, and coarse battery/CPU/disk levels. They stay in memory on this Mac (app history for at most three hours); window titles and contents are never read and nothing is saved or sent online. Because the model runs on this Mac, every chat gives it this context up front (current and recent apps with minutes, active time, time of day, battery, CPU and disk), so questions like “what am I doing?” are answered directly. Turning awareness off clears the history and stops sharing it. Each part can be switched off in Settings → Buntini (Out and about) and Settings → Privacy; Less motion stops all walking.

The companion feels alive without running the model: it blinks at random, picks idle habits that match its motion personality (glancing around, slow blinks, head tilts, ear flicks, grooming, stretching, yawning, a playful wiggle-and-hop, or nodding off), and its eyes follow your cursor. Stroke it to make it purr, click it for a happy hop with hearts, and it cheers when a task finishes or droops when one fails. Cursor tracking polls the pointer position locally a few times a second and never stores it. "Less motion" turns all of this off.

## Voice setup

Push-to-talk uses Apple's on-device speech recognition (SpeechAnalyzer, macOS 26) by default; macOS may download the language model once. With the local MLX brain and a local Whisper model folder, Buntini uses MLX Whisper instead. Whisper and the optional Silero speech filter are set under Brain → Advanced; see [voice setup](voice-setup.md). Runtime never installs packages.

Buntini requests microphone access only when you deliberately press the mic. It listens until you tap again or let go (at most 30 seconds), then sends the transcript. Temporary private audio is deleted after success, failure, or cancellation. There is no continuous listening or wake word. Sentence streaming uses installed macOS voices and can be interrupted, including queued speech.

## Privacy and limits

The full [privacy policy](../PRIVACY.md) lists what stays on your Mac, what leaves it and when, and your controls.

**On-device by design.** The local model may use anything Buntini can see on this Mac (activity, approved folders, system state), because none of it leaves the machine. Restrictions apply at egress: search providers and online integrations receive only the exact request you approve. A cloud model receives your messages, and local data only for the categories you switch on under Settings → Privacy → Cloud models may see: what you're doing, Mac health, your files, earlier messages, things you asked it to remember (all off by default). Earlier turns that used data you haven't shared are left out of its memory, and your own name is only given to on-device models. `src-tauri/src/privacy.rs` makes that decision for every model-facing path.

Rust enforces the allowlist independently of the model: scoped file search, system snapshots, and explicitly authorized web searches. Tool arguments, IPC messages, model context, provider responses, and output sizes are bounded. Private file results cannot be forwarded to a search provider by the model. Remote results and file excerpts are treated as untrusted data.

Conversation threads, task journals (including model messages, tool evidence, approvals, destination receipts and local-data categories), preferences, timers, monitor schedules and run/delivery history, approved monitor-intent records that prevent duplicate creation, audit events, an FTS5 file index, search cache, and request counts live in SQLite under the app's macOS Application Support directory (`app.buntini.companion`). Temporary audio uses the corresponding cache directory.

## Upgrading from Nexon

Buntini was previously named Nexon. Quit Nexon before the first Buntini launch. On that launch, if `app.buntini.companion` has no database yet, Buntini imports a consistent snapshot of `app.nexon.companion/nexon.sqlite3` (including unsaved WAL changes), renames the default assistant name "Nexon" to "Buntini" (a custom name is kept), and records the result in the audit log. The old folder is left in place with a `migrated-to-buntini` marker, so the import runs once and can be inspected or removed by hand. Leftover Nexon recordings in the old cache are deleted. Brave/Tavily keys move to the new Keychain entry the first time they are read; macOS may ask once whether Buntini can use the old item. macOS ties microphone and notification permissions to the app identity, so Buntini asks for them again. If the import fails, Buntini starts with fresh data, logs the error, and leaves the Nexon data untouched. Indexing is bounded and explicit; only approved folders are included, and indexed hits are reopened within their current permission scope before use. Revoking a folder removes its index entries. Previously saved conversation excerpts remain in history until it is cleared.

**Search budgets.** Browser search does not use a search-API reservation. Brave or Tavily fallback and recurring searches use the configured provider limits. Free-tier mode (on by default) keeps provider requests within the app's free-tier preset and estimates no spend; check the provider's current terms. A daily limit (30 by default) prevents exhausting the month in one day. Turning free-tier mode off exposes a monthly cap, dollar budget and per-search estimate. Each provider attempt is reserved before dispatch; failed or cancelled requests can still count. Cached search results use no new reservation. eBay and Duffel are counted separately, and Buntini warns once a month at 80% of a tracked allowance or budget. API keys stay in macOS Keychain.

Model-proposed actions (reminders, calendar events, notes, Mail drafts, Shortcuts, music/tab controls, memory, timers, monitors, opening apps and https links) run after you approve the exact details in a sheet; Mail drafts are never sent. Recognized direct commands such as “open YouTube”, music controls and Mac controls run immediately from your request. “Google cafés in Porto” starts approved research; “open Google search results for cafés in Porto” explicitly opens results for you to browse. File changes, purchases, account changes and sending messages are unavailable. Social APIs, MCP/OAuth adapters, wake words and Pocket TTS remain future work as designated in the TDD. A read-only macOS update check is available only after explicit approval; it never installs updates.

## Apps, actions, memory and the morning brief

- **Skills:** everyday requests are carried out straight away, without waiting for the model, and the reply says what actually happened:
  - *Music*: “play Hey There Delilah”, “pause”, “next song”, “what’s playing?” (Spotify or Music).
  - *Browser*: “open YouTube”, “go to github.com”, “open Google search results for pizza near me”, “summarise this page”, “what tabs do I have open?”, “switch to my Gmail tab”, “close this tab”. Requests about an existing page read it from your browser on this Mac. Web research uses the separate approved flow described above.
  - *This*: “summarise this”, “explain the selected text”, “translate this into Portuguese”, “what does this mean?”. “This” is whatever was in front when you opened Buntini: the text you selected (in any app that exposes it to Accessibility), otherwise the email open in Mail, the page in your browser or the files selected in Finder. If you switch to another app, “this” means that app instead. Nothing selected in another app gets a reply saying so rather than a guess. Once Buntini has answered, “summarise that” or “explain it” means its answer. “Translate this to Spanish: good morning” translates the text you typed. “Remind me about this tomorrow at 9” names the reminder after the email, page, selection or file, and the approval sheet shows the time the model worked out.
  - *Inbox and day*: “what needs a reply?” sorts unreplied inbox email from the last seven days by fixed rules, without a model: a person who wrote to you directly and asked something (or a flagged message) needs a reply; other mail from people is listed separately; automated senders are only counted. It opens the newest 12 messages people sent you directly and says so when there were more, rather than calling the rest “nothing”. “My day” gives the morning brief on request. Both need Mail, Calendar or Reminders allowed in Settings → Apps for the parts they include. On a fresh install, the welcome shortcuts say which app they read and offer to allow it right there (or open Settings → Apps); nothing is allowed until you choose.
  - *People*: with Contacts allowed, “draft an email to Ana about Friday” looks Ana up in Contacts first. If exactly one person with one address matches, the draft is addressed to them; if several do, the approval sheet lists them and you pick one (or type an address). “What’s Sam’s phone number?” reads Contacts too. Buntini only looks up names you wrote yourself, never ones from an email or page.
  - *Replies*: with an email open in Mail (or text selected in it), “reply to this saying I’ll be there” prepares a Mail draft to its sender with a “Re:” subject. “Reply in French” or “respond to this question in one sentence” is answered in chat, without a draft. The model writes only the body, which you review and edit in the approval sheet; Buntini never sends it. The draft is a new message, not threaded under the original.
  - *Recall*: “what did Ana say about the lease?”, “did Sam mention the trip?”, “what do you know about my landlord?” search Buntini’s local memory index: remembered facts, earlier conversations, and the Notes, Mail, Calendar, Reminders and shared-folder text you allowed. It is built on your Mac shortly after launch and refreshed every six hours while Buntini runs, ranking by keywords and by meaning. Updates wait while you're chatting or the Mac is on battery, and never open a closed Mail or Notes; what was indexed from them stays until they are open at the next update. Answers come from indexed excerpts, which may be older than the source.
  - *Mac*: “set the volume to 30%”, “louder”, “mute”, “turn on dark mode”, “lock my screen”, “set a timer for 10 minutes” (saved across restarts; with desktop notifications on, macOS can show the alert even if Buntini has quit, and sleep still delays it).
  Pressing play in Spotify, reading pages in Chrome, Brave, Edge and Arc, and locking the screen use macOS Accessibility; Settings → Apps shows whether it’s allowed. Anything else goes to the model as before.

- **Apps:** Settings → Apps lists the apps on your Mac that Buntini can work with: Mail, Calendar, Reminders, Notes, Contacts, Music, Spotify, Shortcuts, and the Safari, Chrome, Brave, Edge and Arc browsers. Nothing is allowed until you switch it on, and there are no accounts to connect: Buntini talks to the apps on this Mac. Once allowed, it can read your recent email and draft replies, see any day’s events (repeating ones included) and your open reminders, find notes, find people’s email addresses and phone numbers in Contacts, say what’s playing or play, pause and skip in Music or Spotify, and list, find or switch to your open browser tabs and read the page showing in Safari. Private and incognito windows are skipped in Chrome, Brave, Edge and Arc; Safari doesn’t say which windows are private, so close them before asking. Firefox doesn’t let other apps see its tabs. Music plays playlists and songs from your library. Requests like “play Hey There Delilah”, “pause”, “next song” and “what’s playing?” are handled directly, without the model, so they’re quick and use your exact words. Spotify doesn’t let other apps search it, so Buntini opens Spotify’s search, presses play on the best match (preferring the original over covers, karaoke or live versions unless you ask for them) and tells you what’s actually playing; for that, macOS asks you to allow Buntini under Privacy & Security → Accessibility (play, pause and skip work without it). macOS asks once per app. Every other app in your Applications folder can be opened on request, after you confirm. What you read from these apps is shared with a cloud brain only if you turn on that kind of data in Privacy.

- **Actions:** ask Buntini to add a reminder or calendar event, write a note, draft an email, run one of your Shortcuts, or open an app or link. A sheet shows every detail, each field can be edited, and nothing runs until you choose Allow. While a sheet is open the rest of the panel and the pet wait, Tab stays in the sheet, and nothing new starts (typed, spoken or from a nudge) until you allow or cancel it. Each needs its app allowed in Settings → Apps, and macOS asks once for permission to use it.
- **Memory:** say “remember that…” and approve the fact; Buntini keeps it on your Mac and uses it in later chats. Settings → Privacy → Memory lists everything it remembers; remove any item there.
- **Routines:** ask in chat, for example “Every weekday at 8, brief me” or “Every Sunday evening, tell me what’s on next week”. You see the request, the days and the time, and can change them before you allow it. Each time, Buntini does it on its own, puts the result in the routine’s own conversation and sends a notification (with notifications on) that opens it. If a run would change something, such as drafting an email or pausing music, it waits under **Waiting for you** instead. Routines run while Buntini is open and your Mac is awake; one missed while your Mac slept runs when it wakes, within four hours. History → Routines lists them, with Pause and Delete. A routine can’t use what’s on your screen, and recurring reminders and searches have their own tools.
- **Morning brief:** turn it on in Settings → Buntini, or with Resume in History → Routines, where it is the built-in routine. The first time you open Buntini each day it summarises today’s calendar events, due reminders and who is waiting for an email reply (once Calendar, Reminders and Mail are allowed in Settings → Apps), read on your Mac without a model. This automatic brief never opens Mail: if Mail is closed it says it skipped your inbox. “My day” on the welcome screen shows it any time and may open Mail, as “what needs a reply?” does. While it is being put together you see the same typing bubble as a chat, and Stop discards it.
- **Your data:** Settings → Privacy → Your data can export everything as JSON (keys excluded), copy a support summary for bug reports, or delete everything and start over. History → Clear conversation history asks first: it erases every conversation and saved task and the search index of your folders’ text, and keeps the things you asked Buntini to remember.
